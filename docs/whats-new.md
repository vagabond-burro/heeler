# What's new

## 2026.5.1

### New

- **Aspect ratio calculator.** The calculator button at the right end of the crop bar works out the ratio of a resolution (1920 by 1080 is 16:9), holds the crop to it, and saves it under a name of your own. Saved ratios join the crop bar's menu and Photo > Crop / Straighten > Crop to Aspect Ratio, for every photograph. [Image viewport](user-guide/image-viewport.md).
- **GoPro GPR files open.** Raw photos from the GoPro HERO5 Black through the HERO11 Black and the Fusion now show up in your library and develop like any other raw file, though without GoPro's lens shading correction yet, so their corners come out darker. GPR compresses its pixels with GoPro's own codec, which Heeler now decodes. A damaged GPR says so and does not close the app. [Library](user-guide/library.md).
- **Hue stable Curves.** The **HUE** button under the Curves plot makes the RGB curve change tone without shifting color: it moves the brightest and darkest channels and keeps the middle one in its place between them. Off, the curve runs on each channel on its own as before, which adds saturation and can shift hue under a contrast curve. Curves start with it off, so your edits render as they did. [Curves](user-guide/adjustments/curves.md#hue-stable).

### Fixes

- **The Depth Map card shows the depth.** In the Graph, a Depth Map whose depth feeds another node now shows its depth plane as its thumbnail, near white and far black as View depth paints it, with a line under it saying so. Its card used to show the photograph it passes through, which looked like no depth at all.
- **Export layers follow the file you get.** The file name's extension decides an export's file type, but the extra layers went by the format chosen in the panel: a TIFF export saved as photo.png wrote a PNG with TIFF layer files named like PNGs beside it, and no warning. The layers, the Output node's alpha and a transparent background now follow the file actually written: beside a TIFF the layers are written as before, an EXR packs them inside, and a type that cannot hold them drops them and names them in the log. [Export](user-guide/export.md).
- **Curves in Tangent mode renders the curve it draws.** With Tangent on and every point's handles still automatic (none dragged, or all reset), the photograph followed a slightly different curve from the one in the plot. Between the curve's end points it now follows the plotted one. Past an end point pulled in from the corner it continues the way a Smooth curve does, rising where the plot shows it level, as before only if no handle was ever set. A photograph edited that way can change a little; one with any handle dragged renders as before. [Curves](user-guide/adjustments/curves.md).
- **Curves has no step at the top of the range.** Highlights brighter than white skipped the curve, so a curve whose top point was lowered (a faded white, say) darkened everything up to white and left anything brighter where it was: a visible jump in skies and specular highlights, and a color shift where only one channel went past white. Those highlights now carry on from where the curve ends, lowered by the same amount and never clipped. A curve whose top stays at white renders as before. [Curves](user-guide/adjustments/curves.md).

A copy that was activated keeps working, with nothing to do. [Legal](user-guide/legal/README.md).

- **Closing with no photograph open** (an empty collection or folder in view) no longer refuses with "Saved edits are still being read."

## 2026.4.3

### New

- **Depth Lighting's Strength runs -100 to 100, and reaches further.** Each light's Strength went to 200, an odd top for a slider whose neighbors stop at 100. It now runs from -100 to 100 (below zero still emits darkness), and every point of it is 50% stronger than the same point was: the top gives three times the light, where 200 gave twice it. Lights you already set keep their place on the slider, so a light at 150 reads 75 and shines half again as bright; a new light starts at 25, where 50 sat before. [Depth Lighting](user-guide/adjustments/depth-lighting.md).
- **Cancel stitch.** A panorama stitching can be stopped from its progress card, as a stack merge can. It stops within a moment, even in the middle of blending a frame at full size, and stays unstitched until you click **Stitch again** on the card or in the Pano panel, or change its frames or settings; nothing starts it over behind your back. [Combining photographs](user-guide/menus.md#combining-photographs).

- **Recolor shows what its Neutral guard holds back.** Under the Neutral guard, a strip shows how colorful the photograph is with the guard's fade drawn over it, and a line says how much of the photograph the Hue rows reach. On a muted photograph (dusk, haze, overcast) the guard keeps most of it out of the Hue rows, so a Hue curve can move a long way and barely change the picture; when that happens the line says how much the guard holds back, the status line says why, and **Lower to** sets the guard that lets the edit land, one click away. [Recolor](user-guide/adjustments/recolor.md#why-a-curve-barely-changes-the-picture).

- **Drags carry on outside the canvas.** A marquee, a lasso, a crop handle, a Radial or Linear gradient's handle, the straighten line, a layer's Transform and Warp handles and every brush stroke used to end the moment the pointer left the photograph. Now they keep going until you let go, so you can drag past the edge to be sure a selection or a crop reaches it, and a rectangle, ellipse, freehand or magnetic selection can begin on the gray around the photograph; its outline follows the pointer past the edge, the selection is the part of the shape inside the picture, and a brush that runs off the side paints what it covers inside and nothing beyond. [Image viewport](user-guide/image-viewport.md).

### Fixes

- **A baked DNG of a very grainy picture opens.** Heeler checks that a RAW's develop is a picture and not the noise a camera's unreadable compression turns into. A max stack of 432 night frames keeps every frame's brightest grain, looked like that noise, and its baked DNG was refused; with no other picture in the file, the canvas stayed on a low-resolution stand-in, retrying. Heeler's own bakes now skip that check, and a file that does fail it says so instead of naming the TIFF reader.
- **A stack or panorama of JPEGs opens like a JPEG.** The Tone Profile, Heeler's starting look for RAW files, is off for a JPEG because the camera has already rendered it. A stack or panorama made from JPEGs had it on all the same, so the camera's contrast and color got Heeler's on top: the merge looked over-saturated, and a bake froze that look. Now a stack or panorama whose frames are all finished pictures (JPEG, TIFF, PNG and the like, and DNGs baked by Heeler; in a panorama, also phone DNGs that carry their own rendering, which a panorama stitches as the phone rendered them) opens with the Tone Profile off, from its very first thumbnail, and one you already made switches it off when it opens, unless you changed the profile yourself. One with a RAW frame in it develops like a RAW, as before. [Source](user-guide/adjustments/source.md#tone-profile).

- **A bake stays marked as finished when another program edits it.** A DNG baked by Heeler opens with the Tone Profile off because it is already rendered, and Heeler knew it only by the file's Software field, which other programs often rewrite when they save metadata; such a bake opened with the RAW profile on. Bakes now also carry the mark in their XMP metadata, and Heeler finds it wherever the other program moved it in the file. Bakes made by earlier versions carry only the Software field. [Bake to Image](user-guide/composites.md#bake-to-image).
- **A panorama's full-size export uses every frame its preview does.** The stitcher found and matched features in each frame at the size it was given, so the full-size stitch of 20 megapixel frames chased fine texture and could fail to place frames the preview placed: on a real five-frame panorama the export left out two frames and much of the right of the view, without an error. Every frame is now registered at the preview's own size, so the preview and the export solve the same panorama, and the full-size stitch spends less time finding features. Panoramas you already stitched are stitched again the next time they are shown. [Combining photographs](user-guide/menus.md#combining-photographs).
- **Changing a stopped stack or panorama starts it again.** After **Cancel merge** or **Cancel stitch**, changing the method, alignment, frames or panorama settings, in the panel or from a script, now merges or stitches with the new settings, as the guide said; the stopped mark kept it stopped. A change that fails leaves it stopped.
- **Baking a stack shows each step in turn.** Bake to Image kept its dialog up on "Baking…" for the whole bake, so when a stack had to merge at full size first, the merge's card appeared half hidden behind it. Now **Bake** closes the dialog, the merge's card shows (with **Cancel merge**), then the bake's progress with its own **Cancel**; canceling either before the file is being written stops the bake without making one. [Bake to Image](user-guide/composites.md#bake-to-image).
- **The ribbon counts the selection.** With two or more photographs selected, the ribbon's header says how many, in the accent color beside the shown and total count, where before the only sign was the highlighted rows.
- **Panorama progress looks like a stack merge's.** The stitch's card has the stack merge card's layout: the mark, how many frames it is stitching (and whether at full resolution), the bar, the stage with how far along it is and about how long is left, and a rotating dog line. A failed stitch keeps the card with its reason and a Dismiss button. [Combining photographs](user-guide/menus.md#combining-photographs).
- **Merge to HDR estimates exposures the same way in any frame order.** When a bracket's frames do not all carry usable exposure information (the EXIF shutter speed, f-number and ISO that RAW files and most camera JPEGs carry), the merge works out each frame's exposure from the pictures. It compared each frame with the one before it in the stack, so the same bracket merged slightly differently in the camera's order than in sorted order, and it read every 17th pixel, which repeating detail such as a fence or tiles could throw off. It now compares frames darkest to brightest whatever their order, and reads an evenly spread sample of the whole frame.
- **The Stack panel says when an HDR merge is approximate.** HDR divides each frame by its exposure as if its light were linear, which a RAW file's is. A JPEG, TIFF or PNG, or a DNG baked by Heeler, still carries a tone curve: measured on a real five-frame bracket, a camera JPEG two stops over came out 4.9 to 7.8 times as bright as its middle frame, not 4, so a merge of them can be off by up to about a stop. The merge still runs; the panel says it is approximate, names the finished pictures in a bracket that mixes them with RAW, and points to the RAW files. [Combining photographs](user-guide/menus.md#combining-photographs).
- **Merge to Median is a true median with an even number of frames.** With an even count it took the upper of the two middle values, so two frames merged to the brighter of each pair, the same picture as Merge to Light Trails, and four or six frames leaned bright: up to 42 levels on bright stars over four frames. It now averages the two middle values. An odd number of frames merges as before. Median stacks you already made with an even number of frames render a little darker where their frames differ. [Combining photographs](user-guide/menus.md#combining-photographs).
- **An eyedropper click adds a point.** On Recolor's Hue, Sat, Lum, Around and Mask rows and on the Black & White hue curve, a click within 6% of the axis of an existing point (about 22 degrees on a hue row) took that point and added nothing, and a held drag moved it; with a default hue curve's point every 60 degrees, that was most of the wheel. A click now adds a point at the value under the cursor and a held drag moves the new point, as on Curves and Recolor's Depth row; only a click right on a point already there takes it. On the hue curve a click released before the hue was read did nothing, and now adds its point. [Recolor](user-guide/adjustments/recolor.md), [Black and white](user-guide/adjustments/black-and-white.md).
- **Relight's eyedropper adds a point.** Relight's picker changed the nine zone values of older versions, which the curve stops reading once it has points of its own, so on most photographs dragging after a pick did nothing, and a quick click did nothing anywhere. A click now adds a point on the curve at the tone under the pointer (it follows Range shift) and a held drag moves it, as the other curve eyedroppers do. [Relight](user-guide/adjustments/relight.md).
- **A curve eyedropper's point lands on its ghost.** Recolor's and the Black & White hue curve's ghost read a patch half the size their click read, so on busy detail such as foliage the clicked point could land at a different hue than the ghost showed. Hover and click now read the same patch on Curves, Relight, Recolor and Black & White, and a click on a gray spot, which has no hue, drops its point where the ghost holds the last hue it read; on Recolor it used to do nothing at all. Relight's eyedropper also shows its ghost on the curve while you hover, as the others do.
- **The Tone Profile has a switch in Source.** A JPEG, a phone DNG that carries its own rendering, a DNG baked by Heeler and a stack or panorama of finished pictures open with the Tone Profile off, but Source showed its Profile, Baseline and other controls as if it were on, with no switch, so changing them did nothing. Source now has a **Tone profile** switch; while it is off the profile's controls step aside, and switching it on brings them back. [Source](user-guide/adjustments/source.md#tone-profile).
- **Relight's and Recolor's Around eyedroppers aim where the curve acts.** Relight works on the smoothed light of an area, not each pixel's own brightness, and its eyedropper read the single spot under the pointer: on foliage or a cloud edge the point could land as much as two stops from the tone it then moved. Recolor's Around rows read the surroundings over the Around reach dial, and the eyedropper averaged a much smaller patch. Both now read what the adjustment reads, for the ghost and the click alike.
- **Every curve eyedropper takes an existing point the same way.** A click within 1% of the axis of a point already there takes that point, on Curves as on Recolor, Black & White and Relight; Curves took 1.5%. On Curves such a click also selects the point, so holding it drags that point; before, the click did nothing. [Curves](user-guide/adjustments/curves.md).
- **Adding a Lens Character flare keeps your Depth Lighting.** On a photograph whose Depth Lighting held a single light from an older version, adding a flare replaced that light; it is now kept beside the flare's. A new flare's light also saved its strength on the old scale and came back at half.
- **The hue curve's eyedropper keeps its marker to itself while you drag.** In Black & White, pressing on the photograph while a hover sample was still on its way let the next one start mid-drag, and the curve's hover marker came back at the hue the cursor had been on before the press. A press now ends the hover, as it already did in Recolor and Color Sets. [Image viewport](user-guide/image-viewport.md).
- **Eyedropper previews stay on the hue under the cursor.** In Color Sets, Black & White and Recolor, when the app was busy enough to delay a preview, a newer position could be sampled first and an older one after it, so the marker settled on a hue the cursor had already left until the next move. The older position is now dropped. [Image viewport](user-guide/image-viewport.md).
- **Escape in the middle of a drag lets the rest of the app catch up.** Pressing Escape, or putting the tool down, while dragging a crop handle or another tool's handle left the app treating the drag as still under way, so Recolor's Neutral guard strip, Polish and the depth tools waited for some later drag to finish before they updated. Putting the tool down now ends the drag.
- **A drag stops when you switch away mid-drag.** Dragging a Depth Lighting light, a color chart's corner or patch, or the divider between panels, then switching to another app before letting go, left it following the mouse with the button up when you came back. The drag now ends where it was.

## 2026.4.2

### Fixes

- **Every dropdown is Heeler's own.** The Film, Filter and Far menus, Grain's Pattern and Format, Halation's Format and thirty-odd others, 38 menus in all plus the typed suggestion lists, were drawn by the OS, which made them come up white on Windows and unlike the rest of the app everywhere. Each is now the dropdown Source uses, at one size in panels, inspectors and Preferences and at the toolbar's own size in toolbars. [Source](user-guide/adjustments/source.md).
- **Exports declare a print resolution.** JPEG, PNG, WebP and both TIFFs now carry the DPI set in Export, 300 unless you change it, with the metadata kept or stripped: a bare JPEG used to read as 1 DPI and the other formats said nothing, so a layout program sized the picture by a guess. The quick export menu has the setting too, presets remember it, and TIFF layer and mask files written beside an export carry the same. DNG and EXR have no place for it. [Export](user-guide/export.md#dpi).
- **The crop bar reads like the rest of the app.** Its ratio menu is the same dropdown as everywhere else, at the panels' size, and a ratio of your own is two fields, width and height, which show the ratio in force: choose 3:2 and they read 3 and 2. The Split controls beside it come to the same size. [Crop and straighten](user-guide/image-viewport.md#crop-and-straighten).
- **A range mask's node has its layer's controls.** Opened in the Graph, the node shows the same range controls as the Develop layer it belongs to, eyedropper included, and both edit the same settings.
- **The Color Sets eyedropper shows where a click would land.** While a set's picker is armed, hovering the photograph draws a faint marker on the set's color bar at the hue under the cursor; a gray keeps the marker where it last had a hue. The selection itself still changes only while the button is held. [Image viewport](user-guide/image-viewport.md).
- **A stack of a thousand frames merges without freezing.** A large merge works in the background: the viewer shows which frame it has reached, how fast it is going and how long is left, the rest of the app stays usable, and **Cancel merge** stops it without leaving a partial picture; it stays stopped until you click **Merge again**. JPEG frames keep their light and color exactly, and an HDR merge reads each frame's exposure correctly. [Combining photographs](user-guide/menus.md#combining-photographs).

## 2026.4.1

### Fixes

- **Finish adjustment layers correct the picture at its true brightness, so existing ones look different.** Exposure, Curves, Levels, White Balance, Color, Color Balance and Black & White on a Finish layer now work in scene light, as the same controls do in Develop, where they used to work on display values: +1 Exposure is one stop. A layer you made before keeps its numbers and renders differently, so check photographs that use them. Adjustments inside groups read the members below and preserve transparency, in groups made before as well. Clipped adjustments change the base layer's own pixels, including on partially transparent images. Saved numbers stay as entered. [Adjustment layers](user-guide/finish/adjustment-layers.md).
- **Finish controls reach their Graph nodes.** Color Balance has all three color wheels, Levels has its handle plot, and Curves and Levels read the input below their layer for the histogram. Layer effects use shared controls, with Inner/Outer and gradient shape dropdowns and a full gradient stop editor whose stop Alpha fades the overlay. [Layer effects](user-guide/finish/layer-effects.md).
- **Layer effects keep their size at Fit and export and follow the placed, masked layer.** Shadow and Glow extend beyond an imported picture's edges, their frame edges no longer turn into bars, and large shadows render faster. An empty Finish mask closes the layer and its effects. Exported layer pictures include the effects. Blur remains part of a Warp layer when baking. [Layer effects](user-guide/finish/layer-effects.md).
- **iPhone ProRAW opens at the exposure the phone gave it.** A phone's DNG stores its pixels at whatever level the camera's merge left them and carries the rest of the picture as instructions: an exposure shift, a local tone map and a contrast curve. Heeler now follows them while decoding, where it used to develop the bare pixels under its own profile, so bright scenes opened one to two stops over and dark ones up to four stops under, beside thumbnails that were right. These photographs start with the Tone Profile off, and one saved before catches up when it is opened, batch exported or baked, unless you had changed its profile. Brightness and color can still differ a little from the phone's own preview, most in very saturated scenes. The same applies to other phones' DNGs that carry these instructions: a Pixel's opens the same way, and a Samsung Expert RAW, which carries the exposure shift and tone map and no curve, keeps the Tone Profile for its contrast, where it used to open far too dark. A stack of phone frames still merges them scene-linear, as an exposure merge needs, and a panorama stitches them as the phone rendered them. The newer DNG 1.7 form of the tone map is not read yet; a file carrying only that develops as an ordinary RAW. [Source](user-guide/adjustments/source.md#tone-profile).
- **Export's progress line names the file being written.** Under the Export button, the line now shows the name and extension each file lands under, where it showed the original's: a JPEG exported as OpenEXR read as a .jpg for the whole render. [Export](user-guide/export.md).
- **The line Thickness slider has its width back, and its number drags.** In Depth Lighting, Shape Warp, Grid Warp, the Color Checker and the Radial and Linear masks, the Thickness track had collapsed to its handle. It has its track again, and a sideways drag on the number changes the thickness a pixel at a time; a click still types. [Depth Lighting](user-guide/adjustments/depth-lighting.md).
- **Adjustment layers export their sections.** With a Develop adjustment layer selected, every section that edits the layer has the Export box beside its switch, as sections on Base do. Ticked, a TIFF or EXR export carries the picture as that section leaves it on that layer, named for both, such as `Sky Exposure`. To make room, the LAYER and GLOBAL words in the section headers are now icons: stacked sheets for a section that edits the layer, a globe for one that applies to the whole photograph. [Adjustments](user-guide/adjustments/README.md).
- **A layer added after an exported section lands on the picture.** With a section's Export box ticked, adding a layer, removing it and adding another could wire the new layer into the export's branch, where it changed nothing. Layers, and tools added to them, now always go on the picture's own path.
- **A script reads the API's refusal instead of a dropped connection.** When the scripting API turned a request down while the script was still sending (a request past its time limit, an upload too large), the connection could reset before the script read why. The reply now arrives.
- **A directional light has Elevation, Depth and Reach.** In Depth Lighting, a point light had Depth and Reach and a directional light had neither, and its elevation could only be set by dragging its handle. Elevation is now a slider too. Depth picks the plane the light lands hardest on (0 the nearest, as before; 100 the background, with **From scene** to read it under the light's target), and Reach sets how far it carries along depth from there, up to every plane alike. Lights you already made look the same. [Depth Lighting](user-guide/adjustments/depth-lighting.md).
- **Samsung DNG thumbnails use the phone's preview.** A DNG that stores its photograph as one lossless strip (a Samsung Expert RAW does) had that strip mistaken for its embedded preview, which then failed to read, so the thumbnail was developed from the RAW each time. The real preview beside it is found now.
- **The Develop panel holds still while you drag a Depth Map slider.** With Recolor open above Depth Map, each step of an Edges, Flatten or clip drag made a line under Recolor's looks menu leave and come back, and the panel jumped with it.

## 2026.4

### Finish

- **Textured brushes at Fit show the same texture as the export at that size.** Texture, Splatter, Dry Media and Crosshatch average their grain over each pixel, on paint, masks and repair strokes alike, so the grain stays in place as you zoom and stays quick to draw. Grain finer than a Fit pixel reads there as the even tone it makes; zoom to 1:1 to see its detail, which the export keeps.
- **The finest brushes draw at their own size.** A brush narrower than a pixel at Fit paints, masks, retouches and selects at its true width, the same line the export draws, where it used to come out a full pixel wide at Fit, heavier than the export.

Everything in Finish is Heeler Pro; a free copy renders and exports work made with it.

- **Move a selected subject onto its own layer.** Select part of the picture with any selection tool (rectangle, lasso, Smart, Polished or feathered), then choose **Layer > New Layer via Copy**, press `Cmd+J` (`Ctrl+J` on Windows), or choose **From Selection** under the Finish toolbar's **Image** button. The pixels inside the selection land on an image layer directly above the active layer, transparent outside the selection and as soft at its edge as the selection is, so you can move, transform or warp them without touching the photograph; repair the place they came from with Clone and Heal on a Pixel layer below. It copies the active Pixel or Image layer's picture, or the developed photograph when the active layer is an adjustment or there is none. The copy is made at the photograph's own size (if full-size decoding or memory fails, it uses preview-sized pixels and says so in the Console), lines up exactly on a cropped or turned photograph and stays on that part of the scene through a later crop. The selection stays. One undo step. [Image layers](user-guide/finish/image-layer.md#a-copy-of-the-selection).
- **Place another picture over your photograph.** The Finish toolbar's **Image** button (**From File…**, **From Catalog…**) or **Layer > New Finish Layer… > Image Layer…** adds an image layer, centered and fitted with its proportions kept; a photograph from the catalog shows through its own edits and follows them. Place it with **Transform** on the canvas (drag inside to move, snapping to the frame's center and edges, `Cmd` to move freely; a corner or edge to size it, `Shift` to keep proportions, `Option` (`Alt` on Windows) to size from the center; just outside a corner to turn it; `Enter` keeps it, `Esc` puts it back), even past the frame's edge, or type **X**, **Y**, **W**, **H** and **Angle** in the layer's panel. It is placed from the picture's own pixels, so it is sharp at 1:1 and in the export, its transparency comes through, and it blends, masks, groups, clips and exports like any layer. A moved or missing file says where it was, and **Relink** brings it back. The panel's Reset, Keep Proportions, Warp and Relink buttons are icons that name themselves when you point at them. [Image layers](user-guide/finish/image-layer.md).
- **Layers from File keep their look and shape.** **Layer > Layers from File…** now brings TIFF pages and OpenEXR layers in at their true brightness (they were about a stop and a half too dark) and centered and fitted with their proportions kept, instead of stretched to the frame; move them with Transform like any image layer. Layers read before keep their stretched look until read again. [Image layers](user-guide/finish/image-layer.md).
- **Skew, perspective and corner warp.** The Finish toolbar's **Transform** slot has four modes in its popup (hold the button or right-click it): **Transform**, **Skew**, **Perspective** and **Warp**, which moves each corner on its own (it replaces Distort). The slot remembers its mode. With the keyboard instead: `Cmd`-drag (`Ctrl` on Windows) a corner to move it alone, `Cmd+Shift` (`Ctrl+Shift`) on an edge or corner to skew, `Cmd+Option+Shift` (`Ctrl+Alt+Shift`) on a corner for perspective; adding `Option` (`Alt`) makes a skew or corner move even about the center. A drag that would fold the picture or turn it inside out is refused and the status line says why. After a skew the W, H and Angle fields read the picture's edges; after a corner move they say "distorted", and **Reset** squares it again. Each drag is one undo step; `Enter` keeps it and `Esc` puts it back. [Transform](user-guide/finish/image-layer.md#skew-perspective-and-warp).
- **Transform handles reach past the frame.** A layer can be dragged, sized and turned beyond the frame's edge, a corner drag sizes from the opposite corner, and a layer clipped to a moved layer, or a moved layer ticked for export, follows where the layer now is. [Image layers](user-guide/finish/image-layer.md#placing-it).
- **Warp layers bend everything below them.** Add one from the Finish toolbar's **Adjustment** button, under **Utility** (**Warp layer**), or **Layer > New Finish Layer… > Adjustment Layer… > Warp Layer**. Choose its **Type**, **Grid** or **Shapes**: the same grid, shapes, Feather, Twist and Pinch as Grid Warp and Shape Warp in Develop. Opening the layer's settings with its chevron puts its handles on the canvas; closing the settings, `Enter`, another layer, tool or photograph, or leaving Finish puts them away, and `Esc` puts the warp back. Mask the layer and the mask moves with the warp like a cut-out: mask a head, enlarge it with a shape, and the head grows past its old outline while the background stays; where a subject shrinks, the original shows behind it, ready to clone or heal on a Pixel layer below. It warps whatever is below it, image layers included, in stack order, and its grid and shapes stay on the scene through a crop. In the graph it is a node with the same controls. [Warp layer](user-guide/finish/warp-layer.md).
- **An image layer can warp its own picture.** **Warp** in an image layer's panel adds a grid or shapes that bend the picture itself, even past its edges into the **Room** around it, and that move, size, turn and skew with the layer. **Edit warp** puts the handles on the canvas, and **Remove Warp** takes the warp away. [Warping the picture](user-guide/finish/image-layer.md#warping-the-picture).
- **Bake Warp, and Unbake.** **Bake Warp**, at the end of a Warp layer's settings, in its right-click menu or in the Layer menu, turns the Warp layer into an image layer of exactly what it shows, to move, transform and warp again: the warped cut-out if it has a mask, the whole warped frame if not. It keeps the layer's place, name, blend mode, opacity and clipping, and no longer follows Develop edits or the layers below. **Unbake**, in the same three places, brings the live Warp layer back at any time, with its mask and effects and its handles on the canvas, bending whatever is below it now. A bake or a New Layer via Copy that takes longer than half a second shows a progress dialog with **Cancel**, which leaves everything as it was. - **Flip a Finish layer.** **Flip Horizontal** and **Flip Vertical** in the bar above the canvas mirror the selected layer: a picture where it stands, a Pixel layer's strokes, a Warp layer's grid and shapes and a linear Gradient about the frame's center, a group as a unit, with its painted mask and transform. When a layer cannot flip (an adjustment layer flips only its painted mask; a Smart or Object mask stays on what it found), the buttons gray and say why. One undo step each. Grouping or ungrouping an image layer or a moved layer now keeps it where it was placed. [Flipping a layer](user-guide/finish/README.md#flipping-a-layer).
- **Duplicate a Finish layer.** **Duplicate Layer** in a layer's right-click menu or the Layer menu copies a layer that is not inside a group, just above itself, with its mask, effects and placement. [Finish](user-guide/finish/README.md).
- **A tidier Finish toolbar.** The new-layer buttons are now three: a split button for **Pixel**, **Gradient** and **Fill** whose left half repeats the kind you made last (the arrow lists all three), **Adjustment** with **Smart layer** and **Warp layer** under **Utility** at the end of its menu, and **Image**. Group and Delete sit at the right after a divider and move to a second row when a narrow panel cannot fit them. An empty stack lists the same choices with a line on each, and **Layer > New Finish Layer…** lists them in the same groups. [Add-layer toolbar](user-guide/finish/README.md#add-layer-toolbar).
- **Layers open their settings when you ask.** Every row stays compact, the height of a Pixel layer's row; a chevron beside the visibility dot opens and closes that layer's settings and effects, and two layers can be open at once. **Expand settings on select**, a toggle in the Finish toolbar just left of the divider, brings back opening on select; the choice applies to every photograph and is remembered. Adding an effect opens the layer's settings, an effect's Angle reads in whole degrees, and adding a Blur effect no longer empties a layer's effect list. [Settings that open and close](user-guide/finish/README.md#settings-that-open-and-close).
- **Gradient layers color by tone.** A Gradient layer has a third shape beside Linear and Radial: **By tone** colors each pixel by its brightness, darks from the first stop and brights from the last, with as many stops as you want in **ADV**, a falloff between each pair, an opacity on each stop, and the layer's blend mode, opacity and masks. It replaces the Gradient Map adjustment, which is gone from the Adjustment menu and **Layer > New Finish Layer…**; a Gradient Map layer from before opens as a By tone Gradient layer and looks the same. The Gradient Map node stays in the graph. [Gradient layer](user-guide/finish/gradient-layer.md).
- **A Fill layer's color, in its settings.** The color picker sits in the layer's settings instead of a pop-up: the square, the hue rail, the swatches and the hex box, then the color as numbers, **RGB** from 0 to 255 or **CMY** from 0 to 100 percent of ink. Closed, the row shows the color as a chip. [Fill layer](user-guide/finish/fill-layer.md).

A dropdown in a panel or in Preferences opens upward when the panel would cut it off, and the toolbar wraps onto a second row in a narrow viewer. [Finish toolbar](user-guide/finish/toolbar.md).

### Masks and selections

- **Select > Polish refines the Smart or Object mask on screen.** With nothing else selected, **Select > Polish Selection…** opens on the active layer's Smart or Object mask, the one whose marching ants you see, instead of an empty selection that turned the whole frame red. On a Finish layer, **Apply** puts the refined edge back on the layer as an ordinary black and white mask at the photograph's resolution and leaves nothing selected, **Cancel** or `Esc` leaves the mask as it was, and one Undo brings the Smart mask back. On a Develop layer it does what the mask block's **Polish…** button does. With nothing selected and no such mask, the status line says what to select. [Polish selection](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md#polish-selection).
- **Turn a layer mask off.** Shift-click a Finish layer's mask button, click the mask button in a Develop adjustment layer's mask block, or choose **Layer > Disable Layer Mask** (**Enable Layer Mask** while it is off). The layer then applies everywhere at its opacity, the Depth mask included, and the button wears a red slash; do it again and the mask is back exactly as it was. A mask that is off can still be painted, and Export Mask as Layer writes the layer's opacity everywhere. One undo step each. [Masks](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md#add-and-edit-masks).
- **To Mask and Mask from selection make an ordinary black and white mask.** A selection made into a layer's mask (**Mask from selection** in the layer's row, **To Mask** on a Smart mask, **Isolate Selection**, **Fill Selection**) becomes the same paintable mask **Add Layer Mask** makes: the brush paints it, the **Show mask** eye shows it, the new **Invert layer mask** button flips it and the Depth mask multiplies it. The whole selection is used, Smart results, Polish strokes and drawn shapes together, on a cropped photograph too. Shift-click **Mask from selection** to add the selection to the layer's mask, Option-click (Alt-click on Windows) to take it out. In Develop, **To Mask** turns a Smart layer into a Brush layer. One Undo brings back the selection and the old mask together. **Add Layer Mask** on a Finish layer now goes straight into painting the new mask. Layers saved before open with the same mask and look as they did. [Move between selections and masks](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md#move-between-selections-and-masks).
- **Polish refines fine edges at full resolution.** The Matte brush now answers only where you paint and within its **Reach** (from nothing at 0 to one brush radius at 100), so it no longer leaves a soft gray skirt around the subject, and **Refine edge** clears the same haze. **Apply** solves the matte again over the photograph's own pixels, with a progress row and Cancel, so fur stays crisp at 1:1 and in the export; without the matting model or enough memory, the preview's matte stays and Heeler says so. A polished selection turned into a mask keeps that full resolution. **Ramp**, in the Polish panel, a selection layer's mask rows and the Selection Mask node, moves only the soft part of the edge in or out. [Polish selection](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md#polish-selection).
- **Marching ants show the selection that is applied.** After Polish the ants trace the mask the layer applies, every pixel, normally within a third of a working-mask pixel of its edge, so they follow fur, holes and separate pieces; very complex outlines use a coarser display budget, which can simplify small islands without changing the mask. At 1:1 they stay on the photograph while you zoom and pan. [Polish selection](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md#polish-selection).
- **See a mask or depth map as a red overlay.** Option-click (Alt-click on Windows) the eye on a layer's or Color Set's Depth mask, in Develop or Finish, or the View depth eye on any tool that reads the depth map, and it shows as a tint over the photograph that follows the Levels handles as you drag them. **Preferences > Editing & Brush > Mask overlay opacity** (0 to 100 percent, 50 to start) sets how strong every mask overlay is, and the brush panel's Overlay slider starts from it; the tint is now the same color as the brush wash. Switching between the overlay and black and white is immediate when zoomed in. [Masks](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md).
- **A mask eye shows the mask the adjustment uses.** A layer's, Color Set's or Finish layer's mask eye shows the selection times its Depth mask, exactly what the adjustment is multiplied by. A plain click on the Depth mask's eye no longer turns the mask eye off: the depth map shows over it, and turning the depth map off brings the mask back. A Color Set's mask and a layer's Depth mask now line up with the photograph at 1:1. [Color Sets](user-guide/adjustments/README.md#color-sets).
- **Mask buttons in one place.** The **Show mask** eye sits in the selected layer's row of buttons, beside the mask buttons, on every kind of layer with a mask, instead of on a row of its own. A Smart mask's seven buttons (Click, Subject, Sky, Refine, Remove, To Mask and Clear) are icons of one size on one line, at the narrowest panel and 150 percent App Zoom too, each naming itself when you point at it. [Masks](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md).
- **Masks, strokes and selections stay on their subject through a crop.** Cropping again (a new rectangle, another ratio, a turn, or Geometry switched off) now carries everything painted on the scene with it in the same undo step: Pixel, clone and heal strokes, Dodge & Burn, Fill brush strokes, brush masks, selections, unapplied Polish strokes, radial and linear masks, Grid Warp, Shape Warp, and the lights of Depth Lighting and Lens Flare. A masked layer stays inside its mask after a crop, and under Lens distortion, Grid Warp or Shape Warp, because every mask is now read on the picture you see. Image layers keep their proportions and their place when the frame changes shape. Photographs edited before follow from their next crop change. [Masks after a crop](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md#masks-after-a-crop) and [Geometry](user-guide/adjustments/geometry.md#following-the-crop).
- **Clicks and pickers read what is under the pointer.** On a cropped, turned, lens-corrected or warped photograph, and with Finish layers present, a Smart click selects what you clicked, and the white balance picker, a range mask's eyedropper and the depth pickers read the spot under the pointer. Without the Subject model, **Subject** aims at the middle of the frame on screen. Smart clicks made on a cropped photograph before this still point where they did: **Clear** and click again. [Masks](user-guide/finish/masks-selections.md).

[Sharpening](user-guide/adjustments/sharpening.md) and [Skin Softening](user-guide/adjustments/skin-softening.md).

Thickness on a Radial layer, Grid Warp and Depth Lighting can also be set for one photograph, with **Preference** handing it back; that choice is kept with the photograph and is not an edit or an undo step. ### Fixes

- **Brush edges at Fit look as they export.** Every brush (paint, clone, heal, blur, Dodge & Burn, the eraser, mask brushes, a selection's brush and the Polish brush) now covers each pixel by the share of it the brush covers, so a hard edge at Fit is the exported edge seen smaller, where it was up to 15 percent too light or too dark along its rim, and a small or soft brush at Fit no longer looks heavier or lighter than it exports. - **`HEELER_DEBUG=1` in the environment** turns debug logging on at launch, for the stretch the console's own switch cannot reach; the boot's catalog update check and answer are timed in the log at every tier.

- **Catalog backups are verified and journal-complete.** Every copy, the update prompt's, the scheduled ones and Catalog backup, folds the catalog's journal in first and is compared with the catalog before it takes its name; a short copy is refused and named. The update prompt's done line says what the copy holds.
- **A clean quit leaves no journal beside the catalog**, and a catalog opened with a journal beside it is checked for damage first, so a copy placed over it by hand that the stale journal has broken is refused in words that say what happened, instead of failing later in the session as "malformed". The check is SQLite's own integrity check: it catches the damage, not the journal's origin.
- **Restore catalog from copy** in Catalogs and recovery puts a backup in place the safe way: journal handled, the copy verified, the previous file and its journal renamed with the date, never deleted.

- **The Color Checker section is laid out as the work goes**: the chart and its placement, then the calibration and what to do with it, then the chart lines' look. Where the fit's white balance goes is a named two-way choice, To node or In matrix, rather than a chip that read as a toggle.
- **The catalog's folder listing reads in order off an index** (schema 17). This is the first schema change since the upgrade gate was built, so the first launch of 26.3 asks about backing up the catalog before it is updated.

- The depth mask's **Levels** widget follows the right panel's width. It had sized from its own default, so resizing the panel never reached it; the width is now threaded through every seat the widget sits in.
- The **Recolor** eyedropper keeps working after BY switches to a Depth row: the armed picker asks for the depth plane itself, a failed read is said in the log, and the Curves picker re-aims at the channel on screen when its channel changes mid-pick.
- A **directional light only adds**: raising its Strength no longer darkens the far frame, the point lamp's rule now shared by the sun. The depth emphasis still lands it harder near than far, and a negative Strength takes light away by the same shape.
- Dropping a dragged pipe on a card's **mask or alpha diamond** connects: the diamond hangs a few pixels below the card's edge and the drop test only counted the rectangle, and where cards overlap the drop now lands on the one on top rather than dying on the one beneath.
- A node with a field in and out (**Invert Mask**, **Compare**, **Logic**, **Math**, **Remap**, the Export Layer's gray pair) dropped on a mask or alpha wire **splices into it**, the same gesture as the image chain; such wires took no insert at all, so the node simply moved past.

### Catalogs

- Every door a catalog open passes through, the app, the headless CLI and the batch exporter, holds an older catalog at a gate until you choose: **Back Up and Update** (a snapshot written unopened beside the catalog, named `catalog.before-<version>.sqlite`), **Update Without Backup**, or **Quit**.
- A policy file outside the catalog can answer the backup ahead of time; **Preferences > Backup** carries the toggle.

### OpenEXR

- **OpenEXR opens.** The beauty layer is found across the common renderers' naming, primaries come from the file's chromaticities, and values above 1.0 are kept as headroom.
- **An EXR's Z or mist pass is the depth map** instead of the model: the Depth Map section says FROM FILE with the channel's name, and Recompute re-reads the pass.
- **The Object mask** picks objects and materials a render named in its Cryptomatte layers, by list or by clicking the photograph; plain channels read as masks by name.
- **Finish layers take an Object mask too**: a toggle beside the Depth mask on a picture layer, shown only for an OpenEXR, masks the layer by the objects the file names, chosen from the same list or by clicking the photograph; the Depth mask multiplies it like any other. The Depth mask's own glyph is now the Adjustments panel's depth picture rather than the canvas header's before-and-after.
- **Depth Lighting shades with the render's own normals pass** when the file also says which way its camera faces, with auto, camera and off as the choice.

### Export

- **16-bit TIFFs keep their alpha**: a Transparent TIFF is no longer silently opaque.
- **TIFF 32-bit float** joins the export formats: the scene-linear numbers as they are, no curve, no clamp.
- **The Export Layer node** is the graph's way to say what lands beside the beauty: a pass-through card dropped onto any wire writes what it carries with every export, packed as a named layer into the one EXR or written as a sibling TIFF, with an alpha input that replaces the written layer's alpha.
- **Output gains an alpha port**: a mask pipe on the card sets the export's transparency as a wired field, winning over the Matte toggle.
- **A Finish picture layer's Export tick** writes the layer with every export, named after the layer, its mask and opacity folded into the alpha and its blend mode, opacity and group recorded in the file. The tick is a view over an Export Layer node in the graph, so the graph shows exactly what an export will carry, and grouping a ticked layer says the tick was removed rather than dropping it quietly.

### File layers on input

- **A multi-page TIFF is a first-class source**: the File node's Layer field reads `page 2`, `page 3` and so on, and the page's alpha pipes out of the node's mask diamond.
- A layered TIFF from another editor says the layer stack is there; the composite pages are what the node reads, since the layer block is private to that editor.

### Layers from File

- **Layer > Layers from File…** lists what a picked file holds, an OpenEXR's RGB layer groups or a TIFF's pages, and adds one image layer per checked entry at the top of the Finish stack, Normal, 100%.
- An image layer's pixels are referenced from the file by page or layer name, never copied into the catalog; a moved file shows the layer as missing, the way a missing photograph does.
- Single channels never list as image layers: they come in as masks through the Object layer kind, and the picker says so.

### The Depth Map node

- **The Depth Map sits at one fixed seat in the graph**, after the geometry nodes and before anything tonal, and the map is computed from whatever is wired into the node, so a tonal edit in front of it changes the map. Every depth tool and depth mask but Black & White reads the plane by a visible wire from the node's depth output (Black & White still takes its planted raster), and a photograph saved before this loads with the node moved to its seat and its consumers wired, the picture unchanged.

### Color Checker

- **Color Checker calibrates the camera from a photographed reference chart**: place the grid over the chart, Calibrate fits white balance, exposure and a 3x3 matrix onto the published values, and the report reads the mean dE with the worst patches first. A calibration saves under Calibrations and applies to other photographs with a camera warning when it travels; the chart editor builds a custom chart from a palette of primaries and grays or Lab values off a datasheet, and a cell with no target is ignored by the fit.
- **The sample circle is a real setting**: the Sample slider, or a drag on any circle's rim, sizes every circle together and Calibrate measures exactly the region drawn; the chart's lines take the shared line color and thickness, one setting with Shape Warp's outlines; and the chart editor's palette is every patch of every built-in chart grouped by source with a filter, colors type as hex or Lab converting into each other, the grid fields drag to change, and Clear empties a cell.

## 2026.1 pre-release, build 1002 (dbf8f2d)

Since build 881 (dde8262). 121 commits, 7 to 11 September 2026.

### Highlights

- **Noise Reduction has a Model method.** A downloadable SCUNet model denoises the source on the CPU, blended in by Luminance, Chroma and Edge detail.
- **Depth is a mask on every layer.** Adjustment layers and Finish layers gain a Depth block, steered by the same Levels widget the LEVELS section uses.
- **Linked photographs.** Link a selection and an edit to any member lands on the others, with per-section and per-dial overrides.
- **The Takes window.** A pop-out for serious review: notes with room to read, star ratings, and compare.
- **Heeler remembers the window as you left it**, and a Window menu names every part of it.
- **Halation renders in milliseconds instead of seconds**, and the gesture tier renders again.
- **Installers are signed on both platforms.** macOS builds are notarized and stapled; Windows installers are signed through Azure Artifact Signing.

### Noise Reduction: the Model method

- A **Method** row switches Model against the classic pair under one switch. Auto serves both. The row shows whenever the section body does, switched on or off.
- The model is **SCUNet** (Apache-2.0, blind real-noise color weights, the ONNX export pinned by hash). It runs in 256 pixel tiles on the CPU, roughly 2.4 seconds a tile, with the session loaded fresh and dropped after every run so no arena is held between runs.
- The desktop caches the answer per photograph, source options and tier. The preview answer comes first; the full-size run is asked for on purpose or done at export, and once computed it stays on disk.
- Blend controls: **Luminance**, **Chroma** and **Edge detail**.
- The frontend's tile count and time estimate follow the tile size, so the estimate matches what the run does.

### Downloaded models

- **Preferences > Models** lists every model, installed or not, with its size, license, source and version. **INSTALL** opens the same consent card every tool shows and downloads from there; **REMOVE** takes it back off.
- **CHECK FOR MODEL UPDATES** reads a vetted `models.json` on the release feed (one fixed URL, never the API) and offers newer weights for the models installed here. Applying one downloads through the same staged, hashed, verified path.
- Every model carries Vagabond Burro's release mirror as its second source.
- The user guide gains a **Downloaded models** page, linked from the index and from Preferences.

### Depth as a mask

- Adjustment layers and Finish layers gain a **Depth** block, multiplied into the layer's own mask, with an **Invert** that is the layer's alone.
- The block reads the depth map as it is shown: white takes the layer's full effect, black none, Invert the other way. The old near/far/feather window is gone; at its defaults it passed everything, which made the block look like it did nothing.
- The control is the **Levels histogram widget** with its three handles, Black, White and Gamma, drawn over 64 nearness bins of the depth plane. The same widget serves Develop and Finish.
- On Finish layers the Depth mask is an icon button beside the mask button. Lit, it opens a row with Invert depth and the depth eye.
- A maskless layer gets its reveal-all brush mask on the first click, and switching Depth off keeps the mask.
- One depth view control and one glyph now serve all four seats. The mask eye is gone from the Select By range dialogs, which preview their selection with the shared mask and depth eyes instead, trace their ants from the mask, and restore the depth view you had when they close.

### Linked photographs

- **Link Selected** joins a selection. A selection mixing one link and unlinked photographs adds the newcomers to that link; a selection spanning two links merges them whole, and the hint says which. Joining copies no values: a link is a live relationship, and each member moves by the same delta from where it is.
- **Match to This Photo**, on the Photo menu and a thumbnail's menu, is the deliberate sync: it copies one member's edit onto the rest whole, undoably on the open photo. Overrides on either side hold, pinned members sit it out, and warps stay the photograph's own.
- **Overrides** cut both ways. A dotted mark wraps the whole overridden section, link items wear the link glyph, and **Remove override** puts it back.
- **Select Linked** on the Photo menu, and the open photograph's own link lights in accent.
- **Edit Together**, grayed when too many are selected, says the count and the limit in the status line.
- Fixed: landing on another photograph is never an edit. Stepping between photos, selecting a range, removing images and adding to a stack had all been mirroring the difference between two graphs onto the new photo's link group, so values multiplied on each other.

### Bake to Image

- **Bake to Image** turns the current edits into a fresh photograph: a scene-linear DNG with the source's metadata and an origin record, opened on a fresh graph that bypasses the camera profile.
